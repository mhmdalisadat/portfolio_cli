## Problem

Smart people make poor decisions when process is implicit. Urgency masquerades as importance. I needed a repeatable engine, not intuition alone.

## Observation

- Most decisions are reversible; treat them differently.
- The cost of being wrong must be weighed against the cost of delay.
- Second-order effects dominate first-order outcomes.
- Writing clarifies thinking more than additional deliberation.

## Model

The Decision Engine classifies decisions into tiers and applies matching rigor:

| Tier | Reversibility | Process |
|------|---------------|---------|
| Type 1 | Irreversible | Full analysis, sleep on it, document |
| Type 2 | Reversible | 70% rule, time-box, ship and learn |
| Type 3 | Trivial | Default heuristics, no meeting |

<Callout type="info">
When in doubt, ask: *"What would make me reverse this in 30 days?"* If the answer is easy, it's Type 2.
</Callout>

## Components

- **Decision Brief** — one-page: context, options, recommendation, risks
- **Pre-mortem** — assume failure; list causes
- **Reversibility Tag** — explicit classification
- **Review Hook** — calendar reminder to evaluate outcome

## Rules

1. Irreversible decisions require written rationale
2. Default to action on reversible decisions within 48 hours
3. Separate *deciding* from *executing* — different modes
4. Bias toward systems that preserve optionality

## Feedback Loops

**Positive:** Documented decisions → pattern recognition → faster Type 2 throughput → more data for Type 1.

**Negative:** Analysis paralysis → missed windows → forced Type 1 under pressure → poor outcomes.

## Applications

- Architecture choices (database, deployment model)
- Career moves and contract negotiations
- Infrastructure build vs. buy

## Limitations

- Over-process kills velocity on fast-moving teams
- Written briefs don't capture political dynamics
- Emotional decisions resist classification

## Future Evolution

- Decision log with outcome tracking
- Integration with Human Operating Model (state-aware thresholds)
